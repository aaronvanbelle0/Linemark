What Seattle lots are up against

Rain, moss and wet pavement

Months of rain keep lots damp, and moss creeps over faded lines in shaded corners. Worn markings are hardest to see on wet, dark pavement, exactly when drivers need them most.

Tight lots, constant turnover

Seattle lots are small and busy. Delivery vans, rideshare pickups and short-term parking wear down stalls, arrows and crosswalks faster than a typical suburban lot.

Compliance you can't let slide

Faded ADA stalls, access aisles and fire lanes aren't cosmetic. Missing or unreadable markings can leave a property out of ADA and fire code compliance.

Seattle parking lot striping FAQs

How do you schedule parking lot striping around Seattle's rain?

Traffic paint needs dry pavement to bond. We watch the forecast closely for every Seattle job and book around dry windows, with most striping done between late spring and early fall. If rain moves in, we reschedule rather than put down paint that won't last.

Can you stripe at night so my Seattle business doesn't have to close?

Yes. We offer night and early-morning striping for Seattle retail centers, warehouses, and medical offices so your lot is marked and dry before customers, trucks, or patients arrive.

Can you mark warehouse floors and truck courts in SoDo and Georgetown?

Yes. We handle forklift lanes, pedestrian walkways, loading dock markings, and truck court striping for industrial and distribution properties in SoDo, Georgetown, and along the Duwamish, plus fire lane markings to keep emergency access clear.

How long until cars can park on new lines?

In good weather, lines are typically dry in 30 to 60 minutes. We keep traffic off fresh paint until it's ready, then reopen the lot.

How often should a Seattle parking lot be restriped?

Most commercial lots need restriping every 2 to 3 years. Busy entrances, delivery areas, and shaded, damp spots where moss grows can wear faster.

What paint do you use?

We use undiluted Sherwin-Williams PROMAR traffic marking paint for our parking lot line work, applied to a clean, dry surface for the best bond.
